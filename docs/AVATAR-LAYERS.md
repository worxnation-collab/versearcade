# Layered avatar wardrobe

The art for a mix-and-match character: one locked body per figure and every
item as its own transparent layer, all registered to the same canvas, so any
hat goes with any hair goes with any robe. The existing full skins (Moses,
Esther, Gabriel…) stay as **Legendary Looks** that replace the whole figure,
as they do today.

## In the app

- **A spec with an `outfit` is a layered character** (`AvatarSpec.outfit`,
  `data/layered.ts`). `Character` draws it through `components/LayeredFigure.tsx`
  inside its own 120x170 viewBox, so every chip, hero, crowd scene and the room
  postcard gets it with no change of its own. Colour is an SVG filter
  (luminance x colour, clipped to the layer's alpha), not a canvas.
- **A spec without one keeps the old look.** Every account made before the
  wardrobe still renders its starter painting, and the old picker shows a "Try
  the new wardrobe" button rather than changing anybody's look for them. A worn
  full skin still replaces the figure (a Legendary Look).
- **New characters start layered** (`NEW_CHARACTER` in `data/avatar.ts`, used by
  sign-up, onboarding and a fresh guest). `DEFAULT_AVATAR` is unchanged: it is
  also the plain stand-in figure on the public church page.
- **Setup offers hair, top, legs and shoes, free pieces only**; the profile
  offers every slot, with earned pieces drawn locked and saying what earns them.
- **Free vs earned**: `starter` in `art/avatar/items.json` (all hair, the
  everyday clothes) is free in any colour; everything else has a requirement in
  `LAYER_REQS` over the numbers pets already read (level, longest streak, plays,
  verses studied, CPU wins). The gate is client-side: a forged look buys a
  picture, never standing. A piece with no listed requirement is locked, never
  free, so a new item cannot ship unguarded.
- **No migration**: `avatar_character` is an unconstrained jsonb column and the
  only server check (`enforce_skin_entitlement`) concerns full skins.
- **The churchyard draws in this art only** (`features/church/churchLook.ts`).
  A member with a layered character is drawn as they dressed, one in a full
  skin keeps it, and anybody else is drawn in everyday clothes in their own
  figure, tone and hair colour (picked from a hash of their seed, so stable).
  The signed-out public page's plain figures are the same everyday clothes.
  Nothing is stored; it is how a scene draws a member.
- **Nine full skins are shelved** (`SkinDef.shelved`: Moses, Esther, Elijah,
  David, Ruth, Boaz, Gabriel, Michael, Seraph) so the custom character is the
  main event. A shelved skin shows only to a player who has it (or, for a free
  one, is wearing it); nobody loses anything. The overlays, the achievement
  looks, the patron skin and the creator-promo skins stay on the shelf.
- `src/data/avatarLayers.ts` is GENERATED by `layers.py extract`; do not edit it.

- **Three builds — slim, standard, sturdy** (`AvatarSpec.build`, a row in the
  builder at setup and on the profile, free like figure and tone). Slim and
  sturdy are NOT new renders: `layers.py builds` stretches every extracted layer
  horizontally about the figure's centre line (x0.86 / x1.2), faded in from the
  chin to the shoulders, into `public/avatar/<fig>_<build>/`. The head never
  moves, so hair and hats fit as they are; every garment is warped by the same
  function as the body it was cut from, so it stays registered with no new art;
  held items are moved with the hand rather than stretched. Kontext re-renders
  were tried first and rejected: they resized the face and turned "sturdy" into
  a bodybuilder or a belly. **Re-run `builds` after every `extract`**, or a new
  piece falls back to its standard-build art on a slim or sturdy body.
  The full-length frame is a fixed width centred on the body, so a sturdy
  character reads broader rather than zoomed out.

- **The 2026-11-11 drop: 32 pieces in three named sets** (`LAYER_SETS` in
  `data/layered.ts`): Explorer (all at level 7), Lion of Judah (CPU races won,
  12 to 40) and Starlight (verses studied, 20 to 200), plus singles such as the
  Coat of Many Colours (60 plays) and Winged Sandals (30-day streak). A set is a
  name on the tile and nothing else: no bar, no "3 of 5", no reward for
  completing one.

- **The Advent 2026 drop: 11 pieces in two sets, earned on the Advent Road**
  (Nov 29 – Jan 7), the first SEASONAL pieces. A new requirement kind,
  `{ kind: 'road', road, n }`, opens a piece at a waystation; the waystation
  is read from `season_progress` (`useSeason.marks` for roads that are not
  live, `PetProgress.roads`), which outlives the road. Shepherds' Watch comes
  first (headcloth 3, pouch 6, fleece mantle 9, newborn lamb 12), the
  Bethlehem Star Lantern at 15, then The Magi (golden slippers 18, caravan
  trousers 20, casket of myrrh 23, caravan cape 25, robe of the East 28,
  Magi's crown 30). The road's rail and the waystation reveal show them as
  `piece_<id>` (display only, never claimed), so a 1.4.1 player who walks the
  road sees a quiet waystation and finds the piece open on update.
  Gideon's jar was tried and dropped: Kontext would not draw the flame.

## How a layer is made

1. **Edit, don't draw.** Flux Kontext (fal.ai, `fal-ai/flux-pro/kontext`) gets
   the base body and "add ONLY this hat; keep everything else identical".
   Drawing an item on its own puts it at a random size and angle; editing the
   body puts it exactly where it sits on the body.
2. **Subtract.** The edit minus the base is the item, already in place.
3. **Clothing subtracts against the green suit.** A grey robe over a grey suit
   barely differs from it, which shipped holes in the pilot; over bright green
   any garment stands out, and green pixels are discarded.
4. **Tint, don't multiply renders.** `tint: true` items are drawn in grey and
   coloured at runtime by their luminance: ten hair colours from one render.
   Gold armour, wings and crowns are `tint: false` and keep their own colours.

Every clothing layer is also kept to a BAND of the body (`BANDS`, or an item's
own `band`): the model often adds something extra to an edit — sandals under a
hoodie, a repainted suit under sandals — and the band keeps each layer to its
own job. And a clothing layer never keeps the body's own skin (hands, feet),
even relit, or a coloured hoodie paints red hands over the shoes.

Stack order (back to front): `back` → body → `legs` → `shoes` → `top` → `belt`
→ `hair` → `head` → `held`. A back item extracted against the body will include
anything it overlapped in front; that is fine because the body is drawn above it.

## Adding a few pieces to a shipped wardrobe

Most of the cleanup scripts are NOT idempotent (a brim shadow darkens again, a
split skin is split again), and the raw renders of the shipped pieces are not
on every machine, so a new batch runs with `--only` from end to end:

```bash
ONLY=head_magi_crown,top_magi_robe,...
python3 scripts/avatar/layers.py gen --only $ONLY          # raw edits
python3 scripts/avatar/layers.py extract --only $ONLY      # cut; bodies untouched
python3 scripts/avatar/head-face.py --only <new head_ ids>  # add them to CUT/SKIN/CLEAR first
python3 scripts/avatar/green-leak.py --only <full-colour tops/legs>
python3 scripts/avatar/skin-split.py --only <pieces with pale hands>  # add to SPLIT first
python3 scripts/avatar/back-icons.py --only <new back_ ids>
python3 scripts/avatar/layers.py builds --only $ONLY       # slim + sturdy for these only
python3 scripts/avatar/outfits.py /tmp/x.png top,head ebony   # look at it worn
```

`extract --only` no longer re-saves the body layers (body-skin.py repainted
them after extraction), and `builds --only` leaves every other slim/sturdy file
byte for byte as it was. `green-leak.py` is new with Advent: Kontext re-shades
the green suit olive where an open robe or a mantle overlaps it, under
`extract`'s hue threshold.

On a desk machine the fal.ai key comes from the `FAL_KEY` environment variable
(the cloud environment's proxy still injects it there); no file holds it.

## Running it

```bash
python3 scripts/avatar/layers.py gen            # everything not yet rendered
python3 scripts/avatar/layers.py gen --only head_crown,hair_bun --force
python3 scripts/avatar/layers.py extract
python3 scripts/avatar/layers.py builds         # slim + sturdy, after every extract
python3 scripts/avatar/layers.py sheet          # contact sheets in art/avatar/.raw/
```

Needs `numpy`, `scipy`, `Pillow`. The fal.ai key is a network secret on the
cloud environment, injected by its proxy for `queue.fal.run`; no file holds it.
About $0.04 a render.

## Known limits

- **Hats are fitted to a bald head.** On big hair (afro, space buns) a hat
  sits inside the hair. Fix in the renderer (hide hair above the brim, or a
  per-hairstyle lift), not by re-rendering.
- **Kontext ignores negatives sometimes**: it gave the vest sleeves and the
  cloak a hood in the pilot. The prompts now say so in capitals; check the
  sheet and re-roll.
- **Builds are a stretch, not a refit.** Good enough for a storybook figure at
  x0.86-1.2; a much heavier or older body would need its own base and garment
  renders. Ages still need their own bases.
- **Skin tone is not layered yet**: the body is drawn in one tone. The plan is
  the same tint trick on a skin mask.

## Cleanup pass (2026-11-12)

Found on a real phone, each a leak of the LIGHT base body into a layer, which
reads as a pale patch on every darker tone. All fixed in place on the same
paths, then `layers.py builds` re-derived slim and sturdy:

- `scripts/avatar/head-skin.py` — a brim, ring or halo is the one part of a
  headpiece wider than the head, so below the widest such row (inside the head
  outline) the layer is leaked face: it becomes a soft brim shadow. The star
  crown and the halo carried a whole bald head; it is cleared outright. Pixel
  matching (Kontext moves the head) and skin-colour tests (felt and straw ARE
  skin-coloured) both failed first.
- `scripts/avatar/cloth-skin.py` — a TINTED piece is stored grey, so any warm
  saturated pixel in it is skin: cleared, for tops, legs and brimless headwear.
  Full-colour pieces are left alone (leather and the coat of many colours lost
  garment to a hue test), and `top_explorer_jacket` is cut at its hem: it had a
  pair of trousers under it that covered any legs and shoes.
- `scripts/avatar/back-icons.py` — back tiles show a picture of the piece
  (`public/avatar/icons/<id>.webp`): a back layer is only what shows AROUND the
  body, so alone it was a hole with straps, and on the figure every pack looked
  like the same strap.

Also: full-length figures draw past their frame so a balloon or banner is not
cut off; an overlay look (the wings) is dropped on moving into the wardrobe and
the builder shows any one still worn with "Take off"; the goofy pieces unlock
early (balloon, pompom, goggles, skateboard, glow sneakers, trumpet).

## The leaked face under the brim (2026-11-12, `head-face.py`)

The cleanup pass above shipped with a second face inside eleven headwear
layers: the explorer, traveler(s), feather cap, straw, cowboy and fisher hats,
the cap, both bandanas and the female knight helm carried the light base's eyes
and skin under the brim, and the female star crown a whole head. Over a darker
player it read as a pale second face with a moustache — `head-skin.py`
measured the brim from its LAST wide row, and a wide brim droops below the
eyes at its tips, so it cleared the chin and left the face.

`scripts/avatar/head-face.py` (run on `masc`/`fem`, then `layers.py builds`)
does not look for the brim. The base's eyebrows sit at canvas row ~145 on both
figures, so inside the head's outline everything from row 141 down is face and
is cleared, with a short shadow fade under the brim. A helm keeps a wider rim
for its cheek guards; the halo and the star crown clear the whole head below
the skull; the olive and silver pieces also clear skin hue just outside the
outline (a jaw Kontext moved). A colour test alone was tried first and is NOT
safe on the others: it ate the cream cap, the bandanas and the crowns.

The visual audit now wears a brimmed hat, the balloon and a pack on both
figures, because this bug only exists with a hat on — and the contact sheets
from `audit.py` had shown it and been missed. Look at the FACE on every
headwear tile, not only the hat.

## The base's skin and bodysuit, everywhere else (2026-11-12)

The base body wears a CREAM BODYSUIT, wrist to ankle, and every wardrobe
layer was cut from a Kontext edit of it. Four scripts take out what came with
it; run them on `masc`/`fem`, then `layers.py builds`:

- **`body-skin.py`** — the body layers themselves (every tone) kept the suit,
  so a T-shirt or shorts showed cream "sleeves" and "leggings" on everybody.
  The arms below the shoulder cap and the legs below the crotch are repainted
  in that layer's own skin (measured off its hands), shading blurred and
  halved so they read as skin rather than fabric; the cuff line is blended.
  The torso stays cream (an undershirt under an open robe), and the hip band
  is a dark undergarment, so greaves read as worn over shorts and the gap
  under a short top reads as shadow.
- **`skin-split.py`** — 24 pieces carried the base's light HANDS, forearms or
  feet (staffs, crook, torch, fishing net, the mantle and capes, the rope
  belt and sash, eight robes and tops, three pairs of sandals). Those pixels
  MOVE into `<id>__skin.webp`, which `LayeredFigure` draws straight after the
  piece in `skinHex(spec.skin)` through the garment tint filter — so a fist on
  a staff matches its arm on every tone, including ones added later. The list
  (`SPLIT`) is reviewed by eye on `--sheet`; colour alone also flags honey
  wood, gold and parchment, which are the material and are never split.
  `layers.py builds` carries a `__skin` layer with its piece.
- **`hair-rim.py`** — a 1-4px line of the base's forehead inside every
  hairline, which the hair tint turned into a bright outline round the face.
- **`debris.py`** — cut-out hairlines tracing the base's limbs and specks on
  tops, legs and belts (the apron carried both arms' outlines and a sandal).

Male braids and space buns were regenerated: both had lost the hair on the
scalp. **`outfits.py`** renders every piece WORN on a tone, the way the app
draws it — `audit.py` shows a layer alone, and alone is not where a cream
sleeve or a pale fist shows. Run it on ebony and porcelain.
