## Jonah Runs  ·  Jonah 1-4
Hurlbut story 90 · 179 words (about 72-80s) · when: any

**Jonah didn't run from God because he was scared. He ran because he knew exactly what God would do.**

God said, "Go to Nineveh, that great city, and preach to it." But Nineveh was the enemy of Jonah's people. So Jonah went down to Joppa and caught a ship going the other way.

Then the Lord sent a great storm. The sailors fought for their lives while Jonah lay fast asleep below deck. "Throw me into the sea," Jonah told them, "and the storm will stop." They did. And the sea went still.

Then the Lord sent a great fish to swallow Jonah. Three days and three nights he prayed inside it, until the fish threw him up on dry land.

So Jonah went to Nineveh after all. And the whole city, right up to the king, turned from their sins. And God forgave them.

That made Jonah angry. "I knew it," he said. "I knew you were a gracious God, slow to anger, and rich in mercy."

*That's why he ran. He knew God loves his enemies too.*

## Joseph Sold  ·  Genesis 37
Hurlbut story 15 · 171 words (about 68-76s) · when: any (part 1 of 3)

**Joseph's brothers sold him for twenty pieces of silver. Years later, that would save all their lives.**

Jacob loved Joseph more than his other sons, and gave him a coat of bright colours. Then Joseph told his brothers his dreams: their sheaves of grain bowing down to his. They hated him for it.

One day their father sent Joseph to find them in the fields. They saw his bright coat coming from far away. "Here comes that dreamer," they said. "Let's see what becomes of his dreams now."

They threw him into an empty pit and sat down to eat while he begged them to let him out. When traders passed by on the way to Egypt, Judah said, "He's our brother. Let's sell him instead." So they did.

They dipped his coat in goat's blood and brought it home. "A wild beast has eaten him," Jacob cried, and he would not be comforted.

*But Joseph wasn't dead. He was on his way to Egypt. And God was going with him.*

## The Baby in the River  ·  Exodus 1-2
Hurlbut story 20 · 174 words (about 70-77s) · when: any

**A mother put her baby in a basket on the river. It was the only way to save his life.**

In Egypt, the king was afraid of God's people. There were too many of them. So he made them slaves, and ordered every baby boy to be killed.

One mother hid her little boy for three months. When she couldn't hide him any longer, she made a little basket that no water could get into, laid him inside, and set it among the reeds. His big sister Miriam stayed close to watch.

Then the king's own daughter came down to the river. She opened the basket, and the baby began to cry. "This is one of the Hebrew children," she said, and her heart went out to him.

Miriam ran up. "Shall I find a Hebrew woman to nurse him for you?" "Yes, go." And Miriam brought back the baby's own mother.

*The princess named him Moses, which means "drawn out." The king who wanted him dead would raise him in his own palace.*

## The Road Through the Sea  ·  Exodus 14-15
Hurlbut story 24 · 149 words (about 60-66s) · when: Passover / Easter season

**Mountains on both sides. The sea in front. Pharaoh's army behind. There was nowhere left to go.**

Pharaoh had let God's people leave Egypt, then changed his mind and sent his chariots after them. The people cried to Moses, "Why did you bring us out here to die?"

"Don't be afraid," Moses said. "Stand still, and see how the Lord will save you."

That night the pillar of fire moved behind them, bright for Israel, dark for Egypt. And all night a mighty east wind blew, until there was a road of dry land straight through the sea.

So they walked across. When the chariots followed, their wheels stuck in the mud. "Run!" the soldiers cried. "The Lord is fighting for them!" Then Moses lifted his hand, and the waters came back.

*And on the far shore, people who had been slaves for four hundred years began to sing.*

## The Donkey Who Saw the Angel  ·  Numbers 22-24
Hurlbut story 33 · 163 words (about 65-72s) · when: any

**A famous prophet couldn't see the angel standing in the road. His donkey could.**

The king of Moab was terrified of the Israelites camped on his border. So he offered a prophet named Balaam a fortune to come and curse them.

God told Balaam, "Speak only what I tell you." But Balaam wanted that money, and he set off on his donkey.

On the road, the donkey swerved into a field. Balaam beat her. She pressed against a wall and crushed his foot. He beat her again. Then she simply lay down in the road.

And the Lord opened the donkey's mouth. "What have I done to you, that you've beaten me three times?" Then the Lord opened Balaam's eyes. There stood an angel with a drawn sword. The donkey had saved his life.

So Balaam stood above Israel's camp, opened his mouth to curse them, and blessed them. Three times.

*The king paid for a curse. God turned it into a blessing.*

## The Walls Fall Down  ·  Joshua 6
Hurlbut story 37 · 148 words (about 59-66s) · when: any

**The strongest walls in Canaan. God's battle plan? Go for a walk.**

Jericho was shut up tight, its gates closed, its walls high and thick. And God told Joshua exactly what to do.

Once a day for six days, the army marched around the city in silence. Seven priests went ahead, carrying the ark and blowing trumpets of rams' horns. Not a shout. Not a word. Then back to camp.

Imagine the people on the walls, watching. Day after day.

On the seventh day they marched around seven times. Then the priests blew a long blast, and Joshua cried, "Shout! For the Lord has given you the city!" The people shouted, and the walls fell down flat.

Only one house was spared: Rahab's, with a scarlet cord in the window, because she had hidden Israel's spies.

*God won the battle. All they did was trust him and walk.*

## Gideon's Three Hundred  ·  Judges 6-7
Hurlbut story 45 · 152 words (about 61-68s) · when: any

**Gideon had thirty-two thousand soldiers. God said that was too many.**

The Midianites swarmed over Israel like locusts and stole every harvest. And God chose Gideon, the least in his family, to lead Israel against them.

God said, "Too many. They'll say they saved themselves." So everyone who was afraid went home, twenty-two thousand of them. Still too many. At the stream, only three hundred lapped water from their hands. "With these three hundred," God said, "I will save you."

Gideon gave each man a trumpet, and an empty jar with a torch hidden inside.

In the night they surrounded the enemy camp. At Gideon's signal they smashed the jars, held up the torches, blew the trumpets and shouted, "The sword of the Lord and of Gideon!" Every man stood in his place, and the whole camp woke in panic and fled.

*Three hundred men. So everyone would know who really won.*

## Speak, Lord  ·  1 Samuel 1-3
Hurlbut story 50 · 149 words (about 60-66s) · when: any

**A little boy heard his name called in the night. Three times. It wasn't who he thought.**

Samuel's mother had prayed for years for a son, and when he was born, she gave him back to God. So he grew up in the house of the Lord, helping old Eli the priest. Every year she brought him a new little linen coat.

One night, as Samuel lay asleep, a voice called, "Samuel!" He ran to Eli. "Here I am, you called me." "I didn't call you," said Eli. "Go back to bed." It happened again. And again.

Then Eli understood. "If he calls again, say, 'Speak, Lord, for your servant is listening.'"

The voice came again. "Samuel! Samuel!" And the boy answered, "Speak, for your servant is listening."

*God spoke to Samuel that night, and all his life. It started with a little boy who was willing to listen.*

## The Shepherd and the Giant  ·  1 Samuel 17
Hurlbut story 58 · 173 words (about 69-77s) · when: any

**Nine feet tall, and every soldier in Israel was afraid of him. Except a shepherd boy who'd come to bring his brothers lunch.**

For forty days, a giant named Goliath walked out from the Philistine army and shouted, "Send me a man to fight!" No one went.

Then young David arrived with bread and cheese for his brothers. He heard the giant. "Who is he," David said, "to defy the armies of the living God?"

King Saul's armor was too big for him. So David took his staff, chose five smooth stones from the brook, and walked out with his sling. Goliath laughed. "Am I a dog, that you come at me with sticks?"

David said, "You come to me with a sword and a spear. I come to you in the name of the Lord." He ran toward the giant and let the stone fly. It struck Goliath in the forehead, and the giant fell to the ground.

*The battle was the Lord's. All David brought was a sling and his trust.*

## Fire on the Mountain  ·  1 Kings 18
Hurlbut story 77 · 152 words (about 61-68s) · when: any

**Four hundred and fifty prophets against one. Whose God would answer with fire?**

For three years no rain had fallen, because Israel had turned to the idol Baal. Then Elijah called the nation to Mount Carmel. "If the Lord is God, follow him. If Baal is, follow him."

Each side built an altar and laid an offering on it. No fire. The God who answered with fire would be God.

Baal's prophets cried from morning until evening, "Baal, hear us!" Nothing.

Then Elijah had his altar soaked with twelve jars of water, until it filled the trench around it. He prayed one short prayer: "Lord, let them know that you are God."

And the fire of the Lord fell. It burned the offering, the wood, the stones, and licked up the water in the trench. The people fell on their faces. "The Lord, he is God!"

*And that evening, the rain came.*

## The Little Girl and the General  ·  2 Kings 5
Hurlbut story 86 · 157 words (about 63-70s) · when: any

**A great general had a disease no one could cure. The answer came from the youngest servant in his house.**

Naaman commanded the army of Syria. He was brave and honored, but he was a leper. In his house was a little girl taken captive from Israel. She said, "I wish my master would go to the prophet in Israel. He would heal him."

So Naaman went, with chariots and silver and gold. But Elisha didn't even come to the door. He sent a message: "Go and wash in the Jordan seven times."

Naaman was furious. "Aren't our rivers better than every river in Israel?" His servants said, "If he'd asked you to do something hard, you would have done it. Why not this?"

So Naaman dipped himself in the Jordan seven times. And his skin became like the skin of a little child.

*All because a little girl, far from home, still believed in her God.*

## Four Men at the Gate  ·  2 Kings 7
Hurlbut story 88 · 153 words (about 61-68s) · when: any

**Four men with leprosy, outside a starving city, made a decision that saved everyone inside.**

The Syrian army had surrounded Samaria, and the people were starving. Outside the gate sat four lepers. "If we go into the city, we die. If we stay here, we die. Let's go to the Syrian camp. If they spare us, we live."

At twilight they walked into the camp. It was empty. God had made the Syrians hear the sound of a great army in the night, and they had run for their lives, leaving tents, horses, food, silver and gold.

The lepers ate and drank and started hiding treasure. Then they stopped. "We're not doing right. This is a day of good news, and we're keeping quiet."

So they went back and told the city. By the next day, just as Elisha had said, there was food for everyone.

*Good news isn't meant to be kept.*

## The Fourth Man in the Fire  ·  Daniel 3
Hurlbut story 100 · 148 words (about 59-66s) · when: any

**Three young men told the most powerful king in the world, "No." So he heated the furnace seven times hotter.**

King Nebuchadnezzar set up a golden image ninety feet tall. When the music played, everyone had to bow down, or be thrown into a fiery furnace.

The music played. Thousands bowed. Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego stayed standing. "Our God is able to save us," they said. "But even if he does not, we will not serve your gods."

In a rage, the king had them tied up and thrown into the fire.

Then he leapt to his feet. "Didn't we throw three men in? I see four, walking loose in the flames. And the fourth looks like a son of the gods."

They walked out. Not a hair singed. Not even the smell of smoke.

*"There is no other god," the king said, "who can save like this."*

## The Lions' Den  ·  Daniel 6
Hurlbut story 103 · 159 words (about 64-71s) · when: any

**Daniel knew about the new law. He went home, opened his window, and prayed anyway.**

King Darius trusted Daniel more than anyone in his kingdom, and that made the other princes jealous. They couldn't find a single fault in him, except that he prayed to his God.

So they tricked the king into signing a law: for thirty days, anyone who prayed to anyone but the king would be thrown to the lions. Daniel prayed three times a day, just as he always had.

The king was heartbroken, but not even he could change his own law. Daniel was put in the den, and a stone was rolled over the door.

The king didn't sleep that night. At dawn he ran to the den. "Daniel! Has your God been able to save you?" And from inside came Daniel's voice: "My God sent his angel, and shut the lions' mouths."

*Not a scratch on him, because he trusted his God.*

## No Room at the Inn  ·  Luke 2
Hurlbut story 111 · 165 words (about 66-73s) · when: December (Advent)

**The Son of God was born where the animals slept. And the first to hear were shepherds on the night shift.**

The emperor ordered everyone to go to their family's town to be counted. So Joseph took Mary, soon to have her baby, on the long road to Bethlehem.

The town was full. There was no room at the inn. So they stayed where the animals were kept, and there Mary's baby was born. She wrapped him up and laid him in a manger.

Out in the fields, shepherds were watching their sheep. Suddenly an angel stood before them, and the glory of the Lord shone all around. They were terrified.

"Don't be afraid," the angel said. "I bring you good news of great joy. Today a Saviour is born to you, Christ the Lord." Then the sky filled with angels: "Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace."

*And the shepherds ran to Bethlehem, and found him, just as they'd been told.*

## Follow the Star  ·  Matthew 2
Hurlbut story 112 · 151 words (about 60-67s) · when: Late December / Epiphany (Jan 6)

**Wise men crossed half the world following a star. And a king wanted to know exactly where it stopped.**

Far in the east, wise men saw a new star. They believed it meant a great king had been born, and they set out to find him.

In Jerusalem they asked, "Where is the one born King of the Jews? We've come to worship him." King Herod was troubled. The priests told him, "In Bethlehem. The prophets wrote it."

Herod called the wise men in secretly. "Find the child, then come and tell me, so I can worship him too." But he meant him harm.

The star went ahead of them until it stopped over the house where the child was. They knelt and gave him gold, frankincense and myrrh. Then God warned them in a dream, and they went home another way.

*Herod never found him. God had seen to that.*
