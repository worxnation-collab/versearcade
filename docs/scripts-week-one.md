# Script E — the first week of the new schedule

**Records: 2026-10-13 (Tue) → 2026-10-19 (Mon). Seven days, fourteen takes.**

This is the first week of the two-a-day schedule. Every post carries your
voice, so every post needs a recording — but the load is the same as now:
two a day, fourteen a week.

**What it actually costs you:** about **1,260 words**, which is roughly
**nine minutes of talking**, for the whole week. Call it 75 seconds a day.

---

## How to record

**One memo per FORMAT, not per day.** Reading four Tuesdays in a row is easier
than jumping between a book summary, a painting and a prayer. So this batch is
seven short memos:

| Memo | Takes | Roughly |
|---|---|---|
| A · Verse thoughts | 7 | 6 min |
| B · Book | 1 | 30 s |
| C · Moment | 1 | 40 s |
| D · Before/after | 1 | 40 s |
| E · Figure | 1 | 35 s |
| F · Quiet minute | 1 | 50 s |
| G · Prayer | 1 | 45 s |
| H · Story open | 1 | 20 s |

**Say "Day one", "Day two" before each take** in a multi-take memo, with a
clear pause after the number. That is how the batch gets cut apart. The
numbers are the ORDER IN THE MEMO, not the date.

**Read the verse itself before the thought** on the verse takes — the verse
first, a breath, then the thought. Everything else is one continuous read.

**Depart from the script wherever you want.** The words are a runway. The
transcript is corrected against what you actually said, and the AI note claims
the voice and never the words for exactly this reason.

**A `[pause]` means leave two full seconds of silence.** Those are load-bearing
— the reveal on Friday and the beats in the quiet minute and the prayer are
built out of them.

# MEMO A — Verse thoughts (7 takes)

Read the verse, breathe, then the thought.

### Day 1 · Tue 13 Oct · Jonah 3:5

> "And the Ninevites believed God. They proclaimed a fast and dressed in
> sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least."

Nineveh was the capital of Assyria — the empire everybody in Israel was afraid
of. Jonah walks in and says forty days. That's the whole sermon. And the city
believes him, from the greatest of them to the least. I think about that when
I'm sure something is too far gone to change. Jonah had already run in the
opposite direction. He wasn't a good preacher that day, he was a reluctant one,
and it worked anyway. Whatever you're carrying that feels past saving — the
thing you've stopped praying about because you're tired of praying about it —
Nineveh is in the Bible for a reason.

### Day 2 · Wed 14 Oct · John 3:17

> "For God did not send His Son into the world to condemn the world, but to
> save the world through Him."

Everybody knows the verse before this one. This is the sentence right after
it, and it's the one I need more often. God did not send His Son into the world
to condemn the world. Jesus is saying this at night, to a man who came in the
dark because he didn't want to be seen asking. And the first thing Nicodemus
gets is not a warning. If your picture of God is somebody standing over you
with a clipboard, this verse is Jesus telling a nervous man, in the dark, that
that is not who sent Him.

### Day 3 · Thu 15 Oct · 2 Kings 23:25

> "Neither before nor after Josiah was there any king like him, who turned to
> the LORD with all his heart and with all his soul and with all his strength,
> according to all the Law of Moses."

Josiah was eight years old when he became king. Eight. And the verdict on his
whole life is that there was never a king like him, before or after — he turned
to the Lord with all his heart and all his soul and all his strength. That is
not the historian's phrasing. That's the Shema, the verse everybody was
supposed to say every day. Josiah is the one man in the book who actually did
it. It makes me wonder how much of what I'm waiting to be ready for is
something I already know the words to.

### Day 4 · Fri 16 Oct · Colossians 3:15

> "Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts, since as members of one body
> you were called to peace. And be thankful."

Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts. The word Paul uses for rule is a
sports word — it's what an umpire does. Not peace as a feeling that shows up
when things are already calm. Peace as the thing that makes the call. When I
don't know what to do, that's the test I keep forgetting to use: which of these
keeps the peace of Christ in the room. And then he adds three words that sound
like an afterthought and aren't. And be thankful.

### Day 5 · Sat 17 Oct · Mark 9:23

> "If you can? All things are possible to him who believes."

A father brings his son to Jesus and says: if you can do anything, help us. And
Jesus stops on those two words. If you can? Then the father says the most
honest thing anybody says in the gospels. I believe — help my unbelief. He
doesn't clean it up. He doesn't wait until he's sure. He brings the doubt into
the same sentence as the faith and hands both of them over. I've prayed a lot
of polished prayers. That one is the one that sounds like me.

### Day 6 · Sun 18 Oct · Revelation 3:20

> "Behold, I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears My voice and opens
> the door, I will come in and dine with him, and he with Me."

Behold, I stand at the door and knock. I always heard this one as an invitation
to somebody outside the church. It isn't. Jesus is saying it to a church — to
Laodicea, the lukewarm one, the one He has just told to be earnest and repent.
He is on the outside of a room full of people who already believe in Him. And
He isn't breaking the door down. He's knocking. That's the detail I can't get
past. The knocking is patient, and it is still knocking.

### Day 7 · Mon 19 Oct · 1 Peter 1:3

> "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ! By His great mercy
> He has given us new birth into a living hope through the resurrection of
> Jesus Christ from the dead."

Peter is writing to people who have been scattered — exiles, he calls them,
spread across Asia Minor because staying home wasn't an option. And the first
thing he gives them isn't advice. It's a living hope. Not a hope that things go
back to how they were. A living one — and he tells you where it comes from: the
resurrection. Something that already happened. If your hope needs the future to
cooperate, it can die. This one already survived the worst day there has ever
been.

# MEMO B — A book in 30 seconds · Tue 13 Oct · JONAH

Jonah. Four chapters, forty-eight verses — you can read the whole thing
standing up. A prophet gets told to go east, buys a ticket west, and ends up in
the water. Everybody remembers the fish. Almost nobody remembers how it ends:
Nineveh repents, God relents, and Jonah sits outside the city furious about it.
The last line of the book is a question God asks him. And Jonah never answers.
That's where it stops.

# MEMO C — The moment · Wed 14 Oct · THE ALABASTER JAR

She doesn't say anything. That's the part I keep coming back to. A woman walks
into a dinner she wasn't invited to, breaks a jar worth about a year's wages,
and pours it out on Jesus. The room does the maths out loud — that could have
been sold, that could have fed people. She never defends herself. Jesus does it
for her. He says wherever this gospel is preached, what she did will be told
too.

[pause]

Two thousand years later, here we are, telling it.

# MEMO D — What happened just before · Thu 15 Oct · 2 KINGS 23

Right before this verse, Josiah is going room to room through Judah pulling out
the mediums and the idols and the household gods. Not a speech. A clear-out.

[pause]

Then comes the line: neither before nor after was there any king like him.

[pause]

And then the very next verse. Still, the Lord did not turn from His fierce
anger. Josiah's reform was real, and it did not undo what came before him.
That's a hard thing to sit next to. One man can be faithful with everything he
has and still not be able to fix everything.

He did it anyway.

# MEMO E — Who is this? · Fri 16 Oct · BOAZ

Three clues.

One. He owns the field — and he tells his workers to leave extra grain on the
ground on purpose, for someone picking up behind them.

Two. He is descended from Rahab, and he marries a foreigner. Which puts two
outsiders in the family line of David.

Three. What he does for her has a name in the law. Kinsman-redeemer.

[pause — hold it, this is the guess]

It's Boaz.

# MEMO F — A quiet minute · Sat 17 Oct · MARK 9:23

*Slow. This one is mostly silence and music — don't fill it.*

"If you can? All things are possible to him who believes."

[pause]

That's the whole thing. No point to make.

[pause]

The father in this story didn't have it figured out either. He said, I believe
— help my unbelief. And Jesus healed his son anyway.

[pause]

"All things are possible to him who believes."

# MEMO G — A prayer · Sun 18 Oct · REVELATION 3:20

Let's pray.

Father, You stand at the door and You knock. You don't force it.

[pause]

Thank You that You're patient with rooms that have gone lukewarm. Including
mine.

I'm asking for the honesty to notice what I've stopped opening. The
conversation I've been avoiding. The part of my life I've decorated instead of
dealt with.

[pause]

Come in and sit down. Eat with me. Not because I've tidied up — because You
offered.

In Jesus' name. Amen.
